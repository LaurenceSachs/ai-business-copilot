The life cycle of a tick

Most hard ticks pass through four stages: egg, larva, nymph and adult. Each active stage takes a single blood meal, then drops off the host to moult or lay eggs. Most species use a different host at each stage, so they are called three-host ticks.

Example: blacklegged tick (Ixodes scapularis). Cycle spans about 2 years; one clutch of about 1 500–3 000 eggs per female.

One-host ticks: the cattle blue tick

The blue tick (Rhipicephalus (Boophilus) microplus), a major cattle pest in South Africa, stays on one animal as larva, nymph and adult, moulting in place. Only the engorged female drops off to lay eggs.

  • Time on host: about 23 days
  • Eggs per female: about 1 455 ± 435 (lab)
  • Egg to egg: about 65 ± 8 days at 28 °C, 80 % RH (lab)

Lab values; the field cycle is longer and varies with season.
