South Africa · hard ticks (Ixodidae) and soft ticks (Argasidae)

Tick life cycles

All ticks pass through egg, larva, nymph and adult. How many hosts they use, how long they feed and how many nymph stages they have depend on the family. This page shows three ticks common in South Africa, one for each type of life cycle, with the number of days spent in each stage.

Red = time on the host, feeding Black = time off the host (moulting, egg-laying, hatching) d = days · Lab ranges vary with temperature and humidity
Hard tick · three-host

Brown dog tick (Rhipicephalus sanguineus)

Found on dogs countrywide and often completes its whole cycle indoors, in kennels and houses. It drops off to moult between each stage and climbs back onto a new host, usually another dog.

Hard tick · one-host

African blue tick (Rhipicephalus (Boophilus) decoloratus)

A major cattle tick in South Africa. Larva, nymph and adult all feed on the same animal and moult in place. Only the engorged female drops to the ground.

Soft tick · many hosts, many meals

Eyeless tampan (Ornithodoros moubata group)

Lives in warthog burrows and animal shelters in northern South Africa, mainly in wildlife areas, and carries African swine fever virus. Soft ticks have no scutum and their mouthparts sit underneath the body. They hide in the host’s shelter and feed quickly at night, for minutes instead of days.
